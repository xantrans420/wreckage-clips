Build plan · v2 · August 2026

LinkedIn ads to a booked meeting, without anyone picking up the phone

A lead fills in the ad form and picks how they want to hear back. Within two minutes they get a call from an AI agent that qualifies them and books the meeting, or a text with a link. Everything lands in one place, and the system reads its own transcripts every week to get better.

01 — The system

A loop, not a pipeline

The bottom row is the path a lead takes. The arcs above are the system correcting itself: retrying a contact, retuning the agent, and teaching LinkedIn who to show the ad to. Without them you have an expensive answering machine that never improves.

02 — The stack

Nine components, one of them non-negotiable

Everything here is off-the-shelf and pay-as-you-go — no annual contracts, no seats to commit to. Make.com is the one piece with no real substitute, for a reason worth understanding: LinkedIn's lead API sits behind a partner approval queue that runs four weeks at best and four months typically, so we rent access rather than wait for it.

For scale. LinkedIn B2B leads typically cost $50–150 each, so 50 leads a month is roughly $3,000–7,500 of ad spend. This entire stack is about 2% of that. It is not where the money is, and it should not be where the deliberation goes.

03 — Timeline

Six weeks, with something useful live in week one

The sequence is chosen so each phase is worth having on its own. Carrier registration is the only thing we cannot speed up, so it starts on day one and runs in the background while the build proceeds around it.

P0 · foundations2–3 days

  • Start carrier registration — the long pole
  • Buy phone numbers for the first 2–3 markets
  • Rewrite the LinkedIn form: phone, call-or-text, consent
  • Write the qualification criteria in plain English

P1 · capture + SMSweek 1

A lead gets a personal text 90 seconds after they submit, and lands as a row we can read. No AI involved.

  • Lead → validated → stored → team alerted
  • Instant acknowledgement text to everyone
  • Junk numbers filtered at intake

P2 · inbound agentweeks 2–3

The agent goes live on a number people choose to call — lower stakes than dialling strangers.

  • Qualification script, objection handling, booking
  • Full instrumentation from day one
  • Replay harness: test prompt changes offline
  • Conversions API starts collecting

P3 · outboundweek 4

The agent now dials, targeting under two minutes from form submission.

  • Retry ladder, capped at 3–4 attempts
  • Local calling-hours guard
  • Voicemail detection → falls back to text
  • Latency tuned from ~1000 ms to ~600 ms

P4 · two-way + reportingweeks 5–6

  • Texts get real replies, escalating to a human on request
  • Opt-outs honoured everywhere, instantly
  • Meeting reminders
  • Dashboard: contact rate, connect rate, booking rate, cost per meeting

P5 · weekly tuningongoing

The loop that makes it improve. Roughly an hour a week, forever.

  • Read ten transcripts, tag what went wrong
  • Change one thing, version it
  • Replay the old failures before shipping
  • Review what the ads are actually buying

05 — Non-negotiables

Four compliance items, built in from the start

Cheap to do up front, expensive to retrofit, and the fastest way to turn a working system into a liability if skipped. Targeting is global, which means the strictest rule in any market we touch applies everywhere.

  • The agent says it's an AI, in the first breath of every call. The EU AI Act's transparency obligations took effect on 2 August 2026, and several US states have parallel rules. It goes in the opening line, not in a setting someone can switch off.
  • Consent is captured on the form, not assumed. A checkbox covering calls, texts and recording — and we store the wording, timestamp and IP next to the lead. If a complaint arrives eighteen months later, that row is the entire defence.
  • Carrier registration before any US texting. Mandatory, one to two weeks, roughly $6–14. Nothing sends until it clears.
  • One opt-out field, checked everywhere. A "stop contacting me" flag honoured at the top of every branch — text, call and retry alike. One field checked consistently beats any amount of policy documentation.

06 — Roads not taken

Considered and rejected

Worth recording, because in six months someone will ask why we didn't do one of these.

OptionWhy not
n8nNo LinkedIn lead trigger exists — an open feature request since 2025. Better billing model and far better code support, but it cannot receive the lead, which ends the conversation.
ZapierLinkedIn Ads is a premium app, so ~$49/month minimum, and its trigger polls rather than pushes — a known source of duplicated and dropped leads.
Direct LinkedIn APIFree and instant, but behind a partner approval queue of four weeks at best and three to four months typically. Worth applying for in parallel; not worth waiting on.
OpenAI RealtimeCheapest per minute on paper, but it is an API rather than a platform — telephony, retries, recording and transcript storage all become our problem, and cost triples if prompt caching isn't working. That is the four-to-eight weeks of engineering we are avoiding.
Vapi / RetellBoth perfectly good. At this volume the per-minute difference between every option is under $10/month, so the decision goes to voice quality and multilingual coverage instead.
Voice emotion analysis
Cogito, Hume, et al.		Genuinely interesting technology, and the wrong call here on three counts. Emotion recognition is classified high-risk under the EU AI Act — a certification regime, not a disclosure sentence. Accuracy degrades with cultural distance from the training data, which is precisely backwards for global targeting. And at ~30 calls a month, a person reading the transcripts outperforms it. We measure behaviour instead — interruptions, talk ratio, dead air, where they hung up — which answers the same question and is objectively measurable.
HubSpot free as the recordCaps at 1,000 contacts, two users and single-step automations, and pushes to paid within six to twelve months.
Our own landing pageSidesteps LinkedIn's API entirely and is instant, but native lead forms convert much better because they pre-fill and the lead types nothing. Kept in reserve for retargeting audiences.

07 — To start

What's needed from your side

  • Admin access to the LinkedIn Ads account — to edit the lead form and authorise the connection.
  • Company registration details — legal entity name and tax ID, for carrier registration. This blocks US texting, so it's the first thing to hand over.
  • A payment method on Twilio, ElevenLabs and Make. Roughly $95/month, all pay-as-you-go, no contracts.
  • Your qualification criteria, in plain English — what makes a lead worth a meeting. Everything the agent says is downstream of this, so it has to exist before the build starts.
  • The first two or three markets. "Global" is a targeting setting, not a state of readiness — we start where we understand the rules and the language, then expand deliberately.
  • A named owner for the weekly hour. The tuning loop is a person plus a system, not a system. Without someone reading transcripts on a Friday, this is a pipeline with good logging.
